<Info>
  **Before you start**

  - **A running Postern, and the Console in front of you** — everything on this page happens at
    Console → Agents & keys. If Postern runs on a NAS, a home server or anything else headless,
    bring the Console to yourself first:
    [reach it over SSH](/start/install#open-the-console-from-where-you-are).
  - **A few minutes.** Nothing here contacts a source, and nothing here can fail slowly.
  - **A name for the agent you are about to key.** It is what the ledger records against every call
    that agent makes, so name it after the client rather than after the task.
  - **Your password manager, open before you press Mint.** The key is shown once, on the screen
    that follows the mint, and Postern stores only a hash of it. There is nothing to re-reveal.
  - **No source connected yet is fine.** A key is not a connection. Minting one grants an agent
    sectors; whether anything answers in them is a separate question, and a key minted first is
    ready the moment a source lands.
</Info>

<Steps>

<Step title="Name the agent" titleSize="h2" id="name">

Console → Agents & keys → **Mint a key**. The first field is **Name**, and the form suggests
`my-agent`.

The name is the agent's identity everywhere else in Postern. It is what Console → The ledger prints
under **Who** for every call that key makes, and it is the only thing tying a row in that log to a
client you can recognise. Name it after the thing holding the key — the client, the machine, the
person — not after what you expect it to do.

One agent name holds one key and one grant. Minting under a name that already exists is not a
second key: it replaces that agent's key, its sectors and its deadline, and clears any revocation.
That is the rotate path, and the Console offers it under that name.

<Warning>
  Minting under an existing agent name replaces that agent's key. The previous secret stops being
  accepted at once, every token derived from it is deleted in the same transaction, and the live
  sessions it was holding are cut. Only a hash of the old key was ever stored, so there is no copy
  of it anywhere: every client still holding the old secret has to be handed the new one. Use a
  fresh name unless replacing the key is what you came to do.
</Warning>

</Step>

<Step title="Grant the sectors it may reach" titleSize="h2" id="grant-sectors">

Under **Grant which sectors**, six toggles: `finance`, `health`, `mail`, `contacts`, `calendar` and
`home`. The form states the rule underneath them —
`Nothing is granted until you pick it. Tap a sector to toggle it.`

Two properties of a sector decide what you are handing over, and neither has a narrower setting.

A sector is the smallest thing there is to grant. There is no per-source, per-object or per-field
grant, and granting `mail` grants every mail source connected now and every mail source connected
later.

A grant covers **read and act** across every provider in the sector. There is no read-only version.
Today only `home` has anything to act on — Home Assistant serves a live action catalogue, and every
other connector that ships declares an empty one — so `finance`, `health`, `mail`, `contacts` and
`calendar` confer an act permission with nothing behind it. That is a statement about connectors,
not about the model: a connector that grows actions later is covered by grants that already exist,
and nobody is asked again. [Grants and sectors](/reference/grants-and-sectors) is the whole model.

<Note>
  The Console does not stop you minting a key with no sectors at all — the Mint button needs a name
  and nothing else, and the form says so beside it: `No sectors granted yet — this key would be
  denied everything until you edit its grants.` Such a key connects successfully and is then denied
  on every read. That is the intended shape rather than a defect, and it is the reason a connection
  that succeeds can still answer nothing.
</Note>

</Step>

<Step title="Set when it lapses" titleSize="h2" id="expiry">

Under **Expires**, four chips: **Never**, **30 days**, **60 days** and **90 days**.

Which one the form starts on follows this gateway rather than your last choice. On a gateway that
publishes nothing it starts on **Never**; once an address is published it starts on **90 days**, and
the form adds a line saying why. The Console never blocks a never-expiring key — it moves the
default and states the reason, and the gateway's own rule at the OAuth bridge is the one that
refuses.

The distinction worth making is where the key will live, not how long you expect to use it. A key
that never leaves this machine may sit at **Never**. A key that will leave it — pasted into
claude.ai or ChatGPT, carried to another device — should not: it is a credential on somebody else's
infrastructure, and an unexpiring one can only ever be ended by your noticing.

A deadline is a hard stop, not a warning. A key is refused at the instant it expires, including
inside a session it was already holding, and moving the deadline of a lapsed key is refused by the
gateway itself — rotation is the way back.

</Step>

<Step title="Mint it, and put the key somewhere safe before you leave the screen" titleSize="h2" id="mint">

<Warning>
  The key is shown once. It is 32 random bytes; Postern persists only its sha256 hash, and the
  plaintext exists nowhere after this screen — not in the database, not in a log, not behind a
  reveal button. If you leave without it, the only remedy is minting again, which replaces the key
  you just made. Have somewhere to put it open before you press the button.
</Warning>

Press **Mint the key**. The screen that follows is the only moment the plaintext exists, and it
carries four things:

- **Agent key — shown once**, with a **Copy** button. This is the secret.
- **Opens** — the sectors you granted, recapped, `read and act, across every provider in each`. A
  key with none reads `no sectors — this key is denied everything`.
- **MCP endpoint** — the address this gateway is actually serving, and under it the complete connect
  config with a **Copy config · key included** button. That block is the one you paste into a
  client, with the key already in it and nothing to fill in.
- **Done**, and **Connect a hosted client (claude.ai, ChatGPT) →** for a client that reaches you
  from a vendor's cloud.

The Console says the same thing in its own words at the top of that screen: *"Copy the key now and
keep it somewhere safe — it is shown once, and claude.ai or ChatGPT will ask for it later, in the
middle of signing in. Postern keeps only a hash of it; lose it and you simply mint another."*

<Note>
  If a hosted client is where this key is going, copy the key **before** you copy anything else on
  that screen. The endpoint's own Copy button overwrites the clipboard, and the sign-in asks for the
  key at the end of a flow that happens in another app. Witnessed 2026-08-01: the URL went into the
  key box and the sign-in answered `That agent key was not accepted. Check the key in the Console
  and try again.`
</Note>

</Step>

</Steps>

## Confirm it works

- Console → Agents & keys lists the agent under the name you gave it, with the sectors you granted
  and the deadline you set.
- The key is in your password manager, not only on your clipboard. The next Copy button you press
  overwrites it, and nothing on the screen can show it again.
- The sectors listed are the ones you ticked and nothing else. A key showing none is the one that
  connects and is denied everything.

## If something went wrong

| What you see | Where it is answered |
|---|---|
| `No sectors granted yet — this key would be denied everything until you edit its grants.` | [Grant the sectors it may reach](#grant-the-sectors-it-may-reach) |
| You minted, and every read the agent makes comes back denied | [Grant the sectors it may reach](#grant-the-sectors-it-may-reach) |
| You left the screen without the key | [Mint it, and put the key somewhere safe before you leave the screen](#mint-it-and-put-the-key-somewhere-safe-before-you-leave-the-screen) |
| The key box in a hosted client's sign-in rejects what you pasted | [Mint it, and put the key somewhere safe before you leave the screen](#mint-it-and-put-the-key-somewhere-safe-before-you-leave-the-screen) |
| You minted under a name that already existed, and another client stopped working | [Name the agent](#name-the-agent) |
| An agent that worked yesterday is refused today, with nothing changed | [Set when it lapses](#set-when-it-lapses) |
| The mint screen shows no MCP endpoint to copy | [Connect an agent](/start/connect-an-agent) |

## What you have now

One agent, named, holding a key that opens exactly the sectors you ticked and nothing else, until
the deadline you set. Postern holds a hash of that key and can never show it to you again.

Nothing is connected to it yet. The key is a credential from this moment: it belongs wherever your
other secrets live, and every config file you paste it into becomes a file holding a secret. If it
is ever loose, revoking or rotating this agent ends it and every token derived from it at once, and
cuts the sessions it was holding — no source credential is touched, and nothing upstream changes.
