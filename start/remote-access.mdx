description: "The Console stays on the box and you reach it over SSH. Agents reach Postern over your tailnet, and hosted clients over one public port. Both are opt-in."

<Info>
  **Before you start**

  - **Tailscale, and Funnel only if a hosted client needs you** — the two tiers below, in that order.
  - **About 20 minutes, once.**
  - **Off by default** — Postern publishes nothing until you publish it, and both tiers can be taken back down.
  - **A Tailscale account** — the free Personal plan covers a household, and it is the one account you need that is not already yours. Use Tailscale: it is end-to-end encrypted, needs no port forwarding, works behind CGNAT, and the TLS keys never leave your machine. There is no Cloudflare lane here on purpose — a fronting CDN that decrypts at its edge would put your mail and money in plaintext on someone else's network, and that trade is refused by design.
  - **Admin access to Tailscale's admin console** — you will edit the DNS page, and the policy file.
  - **SSH access to the box** — only for the Console tunnel, and only if Postern runs somewhere else.
  - **Postern already installed and running** on the box you are publishing.
  - **Ports taken** — 8443 and 10000 on your tailnet; 443 on the public internet, and only if you go as far as the public tier.
  - **An agent key is shown once**, when you connect a hosted client. Open your password manager before you start that step.
</Info>

## Choose your path

Reaching the Console yourself and reaching Postern from an agent are different
problems with different answers. Take the row that matches what you are trying
to do.

| If you want to… | Then |
|---|---|
| open the Console when Postern runs on a NAS, a home server, or anything headless | [Reach the Console over SSH](#reach-the-console-over-ssh-when-it-runs-on-another-box). Nothing is published. |
| let agents on your own devices reach Postern — a local harness, your phone's Apple Health pushes | install Tailscale, then [the private tier](#serve-the-two-ports-to-your-own-devices). Still nothing public. |
| let a hosted client reach Postern — claude.ai, ChatGPT | the private tier first, then [the public tier](#publish-one-port-on-443-for-hosted-clients) and everything after it |

<Steps>

<Step title="Reach the Console over SSH when it runs on another box" titleSize="h2" id="ssh-tunnel">

The Console never leaves the machine — that is the security model, not a
limitation. It binds loopback, so a browser on any other machine cannot reach
it at all. To use it from your laptop, tunnel loopback over SSH:

```bash
ssh -N -L 127.0.0.1:8787:127.0.0.1:8787 <your-box>
```

Substitute your own host for `<your-box>`. Leave that terminal open, then open
`http://localhost:8787` in your own browser. The Console answers as if it were
running on your laptop, because as far as the box is concerned the request
still arrives on loopback.

Everything from here on is about the **agent-facing** edges. The Console stays
on the box either way, and nothing in the rest of this page changes how you
reach it. If all you needed was the Console, you are done.

</Step>

<Step title="Install Tailscale and turn on the two switches it needs" titleSize="h2" id="tailscale-switches">

Tailscale goes on the box running Postern and on every device you want to reach
it from. Then, in Tailscale's admin console, open the DNS page — both switches
live there — and turn on MagicDNS and HTTPS certificates. Everything below
requires both. Both are silent when missing: the port never answers, and no log
anywhere names the cause.

<Warning>
  Enabling HTTPS certificates publishes the machine's name to the public
  Certificate Transparency ledger. That is a permanent public record and there
  is no way to withdraw it. Name the box blandly before you turn certificates
  on, and never treat the name as a defence.
</Warning>

Your box's name is on the admin console's Machines page, or from `tailscale status`
on the box itself. You need it for every command and every probe below.

</Step>

<Step title="Serve the two ports to your own devices" titleSize="h2" id="private-tier">

Two commands publish the ingest endpoint and the MCP edge to your tailnet only.
Anything that only your own devices need should stay on this tier forever.

<Warning>
  Postern's admin and Console routes carry no per-request authentication.
  Reachability is the authentication — the edge binds 127.0.0.1 and anything
  that can reach those routes is treated as the operator. Publishing port 8787
  to a network, whole, is not a supported configuration, and no password screen
  stands behind it. The first command below is path-scoped for exactly this
  reason: it serves `/api/ingest` and nothing else off that port.
</Warning>

Run both from the box:

```bash
tailscale serve --bg --https=8443 --set-path=/api/ingest http://127.0.0.1:8787/api/ingest
tailscale serve --bg --https=10000 http://127.0.0.1:8788
```

Your phone's Apple Health automation now pushes to
`https://<host>.ts.net:8443/api/ingest/<connection-id>`, and every agent on your
own devices reaches `https://<host>.ts.net:10000/mcp` with its key. There is no
public exposure of any kind.

<Note>
  On a Mac, the GUI and App Store builds never put `tailscale` on your PATH —
  `which tailscale` returns nothing. The CLI lives inside the bundle at
  `/Applications/Tailscale.app/Contents/MacOS/Tailscale`, with a capital T. Set
  `TS=/Applications/Tailscale.app/Contents/MacOS/Tailscale` once and prefix every
  command on this page with `$TS`. Linux and the open-source `tailscaled`
  packages put it on PATH.
</Note>

Verify from another tailnet device before you move on. The first probe must
answer **404** and the second must answer **401** — a 404 is what path-scoping
looks like from outside, and a 401 means the route is alive and asking for the
secret it should ask for:

```bash
curl -so /dev/null -w '%{http_code}' https://gateway.example-tailnet.ts.net:8443/api/agents # MUST be 404 — a 200 means the admin surface is exposed: STOP, re-run the serve commands exactly
curl -so /dev/null -w '%{http_code}' https://gateway.example-tailnet.ts.net:8443/api/ingest/YOUR-ID # MUST be 401 — route alive, secret required
```

If neither answers at all, go back to
[Install Tailscale and turn on the two switches it needs](#install-tailscale-and-turn-on-the-two-switches-it-needs):
a missing MagicDNS or certificates switch fails exactly this silently.

</Step>

<Step title="Publish one port on 443 for hosted clients" titleSize="h2" id="public-tier">

claude.ai and ChatGPT connect from the vendor's own cloud. They can never reach
a tailnet, and they egress on port 443 only. This step is the only reason to
publish anything to the internet; skip it if no hosted client is involved.

Publish the MCP edge — never the REST edge on 8787.

<Warning>
  Arm this once, deliberately. Churning `serve` and `funnel` configuration
  re-issues certificates and can hit Let's Encrypt issuance limits, at which
  point you are locked out of your own hostname for hours with no way to hurry
  it. Get the command right, run it once, and do your retrying in the off-tailnet
  probes further down, never here.
</Warning>

```bash
tailscale funnel --bg --https=443 http://127.0.0.1:8788
tailscale funnel status   # the 443 line must read (Funnel on); every other line (tailnet only)
```

<Note>
  A Funnel on any port but 443 gets a silent TCP reset from hosted clients — the
  connector reports that it could not reach you, and your logs show nothing,
  because nothing ever arrived. And in your tailnet policy, the Access Controls
  file in Tailscale's admin console, scope the funnel node attribute to the
  gateway's tag rather than the whole tailnet: `serve` and `funnel` are
  last-command-wins per port, so scoping means a mistyped command on some other
  machine can never publish anything.
</Note>

Then tell Postern the address it now answers on. Console → Settings → Remote
access → Public → **Set the address**, and save the bare origin: scheme and
hostname, nothing after. No path — the gateway refuses one, and its refusal
names the origin to save instead — and no port, never `:443`, because the value
is echoed verbatim into the OAuth discovery documents, where an exact string
match decides.

<Frame caption="Console → Settings → Remote access. Public is off until you set an address, and the OAuth bridge follows it — there is no separate switch.">
  <img src="/images/remote-console-card.png" alt="The Console's Settings page. Under the heading Remote access, three rows: one labelled Tailnet, noting that serve is set on the box and the Console is not told the live state; one labelled Public, reading off, with a Set the address button; and one labelled OAuth bridge, reading off, with the note that this edge is bearer-only." />
</Frame>

Finally, restart the gateway from the install folder:

```bash
docker compose restart app
```

The restart is not optional. The bridge reads the published address once, at
boot, so until you restart, the gateway is still serving the address it booted
with. The bridge is on exactly when an address is published and off otherwise;
there is no separate flag.

</Step>

<Step title="Probe from a device that is off your tailnet" titleSize="h2" id="verify-off-tailnet">

Probe from a device that is not on your tailnet — a phone on cellular does it.
From inside, you cannot tell Funnel from serve, so an inside probe proves
nothing. Substitute your own hostname. The three answers must read **200**,
**401** and **404** in that order:

```bash
curl -so /dev/null -w '%{http_code}' https://gateway.example-tailnet.ts.net/.well-known/oauth-protected-resource/mcp   # 200 — substitute your own name
curl -so /dev/null -w '%{http_code}' https://gateway.example-tailnet.ts.net/mcp          # 401 — alive, credential required
curl -so /dev/null -w '%{http_code}' https://gateway.example-tailnet.ts.net/api/agents   # 404 — the admin surface is NOT here
```

Only when all three read correctly do you touch any connector UI.

<Note>
  claude.ai caches discovery verdicts, failures included, for about five
  minutes. A fix that does not appear to take often has taken. Fix it, wait,
  then try once — retrying inside the window tells you nothing and tempts you
  into changing a second thing.
</Note>

</Step>

<Step title="Connect the hosted client and sign in with a key" titleSize="h2" id="connect-hosted-client">

In claude.ai: Settings → Connectors → Add custom connector. ChatGPT takes a
longer path, walked end to end in [Connect ChatGPT](/connect/chatgpt).

The URL you paste is your published origin plus `/mcp`. The address Postern
saved is the bare origin, and the address you hand a client adds exactly that
suffix; the Console's hosted-connect card shows the exact value to copy.

When the client asks you to sign in, the login is an agent key. Mint one in the
Console under Agents → new: name it, tick the sectors it may reach, and set an
expiry, because a key that lives in someone else's cloud must be able to end
itself. The key shows once — stash it before you copy anything else, because
there is nothing to re-reveal later, only re-mint. Revoking it afterwards
refuses every token derived from it: the grant is re-read on every call, so the
next call that client makes is denied.

</Step>

<Step title="Take it down, when you want it off" titleSize="h2" id="take-it-down">

Turning off the public tier takes three acts, and any two of them without the
third leaves you worse off than either end state: the Funnel down but the address
still saved leaves a bridge advertising an origin nothing can reach, and the
address cleared but the Funnel still up leaves a public surface of 404s. Skipping
only the restart leaves the gateway serving the old address regardless of what
the Console now shows.

Take down the Funnel, clear the address in Console → Settings → Remote access,
then restart:

```bash
tailscale funnel --https=443 off
docker compose restart app
```

Then confirm nothing is left standing. The output must contain no `AllowFunnel`
key at all:

```bash
tailscale serve status --json
```

The private tier is untouched by all of this and stays up.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Confirm it works

Each item is observable, and each is the thing most likely to be skipped.

- **Tailscale's admin console → DNS**: MagicDNS is on and HTTPS certificates are on. Both, not one.
- **From another tailnet device**: the two probes under [Serve the two ports to your own devices](#serve-the-two-ports-to-your-own-devices) read 404 then 401. A 200 on the first means the admin surface is exposed — stop and re-run the serve commands exactly.
- **`tailscale funnel status`**: the 443 line reads `(Funnel on)`, and every other line reads `(tailnet only)`.
- **From a device off your tailnet**: the three probes under [Probe from a device that is off your tailnet](#probe-from-a-device-that-is-off-your-tailnet) read 200, 401, 404. A device on your tailnet cannot prove this — it cannot tell Funnel from serve.

## What you have now

If you stopped after the SSH tunnel, nothing is published: the Console is
reachable from your laptop for as long as the tunnel is open, and from nowhere
else.

If you took the private tier, your own devices reach the ingest endpoint and the
MCP edge over your tailnet, and the internet reaches nothing. That is where most
setups should stay.

If you took the public tier, what the internet can now reach is precisely eight
routes on one port: three static discovery documents carrying no user data, a
sign-in page, the sign-in POST that an agent key gates, a PKCE-bound token
exchange, one rate-limited registration write, and `/mcp` itself — bearer-gated,
your data behind it. Nothing else on the box is published, and the Console is not
among those eight.

The standing cost is yours: a public hostname in a permanent certificate log, a
Tailscale account to keep, and an address that stays live until you
[take it down](#take-it-down-when-you-want-it-off).
