---
title: "Connect iCloud"
description: "Three read-only connections — mail, calendar, contacts — from one app-specific password, over IMAP, CalDAV and CardDAV. No app to register, nothing to enable."
---

<Info>
  **Before you start**

  - **An Apple Account with two-factor authentication already on.** App-specific
    passwords do not exist without it, and nothing on Postern's side can add one
    for you.
  - **About five minutes.**
  - **One app-specific password**, generated at Apple and pasted here. It is the
    only iCloud credential Postern ever holds — your Apple Account password is
    never asked for and never stored.
  - **Apple shows that password once**, on the screen that generates it. Open
    your password manager before you start.
  - **Postern installed, with the Console in front of you.** The Console answers
    on `127.0.0.1:8787` and only on the machine Postern runs on, so if it lives
    on a NAS or a home server, bring it to yourself over SSH first:
    [Reach the Console over SSH](/start/remote-access#reach-the-console-over-ssh-when-it-runs-on-another-box).
  - **Outbound TLS from that machine.** The gateway connects out to
    `imap.mail.me.com:993`, `caldav.icloud.com` and `contacts.icloud.com`.
    Nothing has to reach you: iCloud is pulled, and no port is published for it.
  - **Three connections, or none.** One credential always creates mail, calendar
    and contacts together, in one transaction. iCloud has no per-sector scopes,
    so there is nothing to pick and no way to take only one of the three.
  - **Read-only.** Postern reads iCloud and never writes to it — no sending, no
    editing an event, no adding a contact.
</Info>

<Steps>

<Step title="Generate an app-specific password at Apple" titleSize="h2" id="app-password">

This part happens entirely at Apple. Sign in at
[account.apple.com](https://account.apple.com/), then open **Sign-In and
Security → App-Specific Passwords**. That section only exists on an account with
two-factor authentication turned on; if it is not on the page, two-factor is off,
and turning it on is what puts it there. Apple's own instructions name
`account.apple.com` and the Console's on-screen steps still name the older
`appleid.apple.com` — if one of them does not land you on your account page, use
the other.

<Warning>
  The password is shown once, on the screen that generates it, and there is no
  way to read it back afterwards. If you lose it, the only route forward is to
  generate a second one and paste that instead — the lost one cannot be
  recovered, only removed. Have somewhere to put it open before you press
  Generate.
</Warning>

Generate an app-specific password and name it `Postern`. The name is for you
alone: it is how you find this one in Apple's list when you want to remove it,
and it is never sent anywhere. Apple prints the password as four groups of four
characters — `xxxx-xxxx-xxxx-xxxx` — and that whole string, hyphens included, is
what the Console takes. Copy it now.

</Step>

<Step title="Paste it into the Console with your Apple ID" titleSize="h2" id="paste">

In the Console, go to **Sources → Add a source → iCloud**. It is the first card
under *Paste a token*. The card takes two things: **Apple ID**, which is the
address you sign in to Apple with, and **App-specific password**, which is
masked as you type it.

<Warning>
  Paste the app-specific password, never your Apple Account password. Postern
  does not check which one you gave it — it seals whatever you paste into the
  local vault, so a primary password ends up stored on your machine and then
  fails anyway, because Apple refuses a primary password for IMAP and DAV on a
  two-factor account. If you have already pasted one, generate an app-specific
  password and paste that over it. And if you decide to change that primary
  password at Apple afterwards, change it **before** you generate the
  app-specific password, because a password change revokes every app-specific
  password on the account.
</Warning>

With both fields filled, press **Connect iCloud**. The Console reports
`iCloud connected.` and moves you to Sources, where a single iCloud group now
holds three connections — mail, calendar and contacts — behind one credential.

That message means the password is sealed and the three connections exist. It
does not mean Apple accepted it: this step never contacts Apple. The first sync
is the test, and it is the next step.

<Note>
  Both fields are shape-checked in the browser before anything leaves the
  machine. An Apple ID that is not an email address is refused with
  `That doesn’t look like an Apple ID — it should be an email like you@icloud.com.`
  An empty password field is refused with
  `Paste the app-specific password you generated — four groups of four. Postern never sees your Apple ID password, only this one.`
  Both are Postern's own refusals rather than Apple's — nothing has been sent
  yet. Correct the field and press the button again.
</Note>

</Step>

<Step title="Watch the first sync land" titleSize="h2" id="first-sync">

In the Console, go to **Sources → the iCloud group** and open any of the three
connections. Each one starts at **Awaiting first sync**. You do not have to wait
out the poll cadence for that first run: the gateway re-scans for new
connections about once a minute, and a connection that has never synced runs
almost as soon as it is seen. The label moves to **Syncing…** with a running
count while the first pass fills, and then to nothing at all.

**There is no healthy badge.** A working connection shows no status word — the
absence is the signal, and anything that appears in that slot (Stale,
Unreachable, Error, Paused) is telling you about a problem.

The first pass is the long one, and it is worth knowing what it does and does
not collect. Mail fetches envelopes only — sender, subject, dates, flags — from
**INBOX and Sent**, and no other folder; bodies and attachments are never
stored, and are fetched from Apple on demand when an agent asks for a specific
message. It reaches back 180 days by default, which the connection's **Recency
window** control sets to 3, 6 or 12 months — changing that clears the
connection's cursor, so the following run re-reads the whole window from
scratch.

Calendar takes every calendar the account serves over CalDAV, and expands
repeating events into individual occurrences from 90 days back to about 400 days
ahead; a repeating event further out than that is not written until the event
next changes. Contacts takes every address book. On a large mailbox the first
pass runs for minutes.

If you would rather not wait for the scheduler, press **Sync now** on any of the
three — and know what it will tell you. The gateway waits thirty seconds for
that run to finish and then answers that the sync is still running; the Console
reads a still-running answer the same way it reads a finished one and reports
`Synced — 0 updated.` On a first pass over a real mailbox that message means the
run is still going, not that there was nothing to fetch. The count under
**Envelopes cached** is the honest signal — reload the page and watch it climb.

<Note>
  All three connections turn to **Error**, with `icloud-mail: sync failed` on the
  mail row and `apple: DAV connect failed` or `apple: DAV account discovery
  failed` on calendar and contacts. Those messages are deliberately blunt:
  the connectors mask anything the upstream said so that a password can never
  reach a log, which means the message names the stage, not the cause. The cause
  is nearly always the credential — either the paste was never checked and the
  password was wrong from the start, or Apple has since revoked it. **Any change
  to your Apple Account password revokes every app-specific password you hold,
  at once and without notice**, so three connections that stopped on the same
  day after weeks of working is that, and nothing else. The way back is a fresh
  password, not the connection page: **Sync now** is disabled while a connection
  is in Error, with *Syncing is held until the connection is restored* beside
  it. Generate a new app-specific password at Apple, return to **Sources → Add a
  source → iCloud**, tick **Yes — replace the stored app-specific password**,
  and paste it. That replaces the stored credential and puts all three
  connections back to active; it never creates a second copy.
</Note>

</Step>

</Steps>

## Confirm it works

Each of these is visible in the Console, and each is a place this connection
quietly does less than it looks like it does.

- **Sources holds one iCloud group with three connections in it** — mail,
  calendar and contacts, under a single credential. They are created in one
  transaction, so the count is three or nothing.
- **None of the three shows a status word.** That slot is empty when a
  connection is healthy, and a word in it — Stale, Unreachable, Error — is the
  only thing it ever says.
- **Each connection reports a `Last synced` clock, a `Next poll` of about ten
  minutes, and a count under `Envelopes cached`, `Events cached` or
  `Contacts cached`.** A zero count with a healthy clock is a real answer, not a
  failure: it means the account has nothing inside the window that connection
  covers.
- **An agent whose key already carries mail, calendar or contacts can read
  them now.** Sectors are granted when a key is minted, not here, so a key that
  already holds those three needs nothing further —
  [Connect an agent](/start/connect-an-agent) covers the other direction.

## What you have now

Three read-only connections behind one credential: mail envelopes, calendar
events and contacts, kept current by a poll about every ten minutes that cannot
be driven faster than every five. Apple publishes no rate limits for these
protocols, so that cadence is deliberately conservative, and a collection that
has not changed costs one cheap probe rather than a full read. The cadence is
per connection, on the connection's own page.

Any agent whose key carries mail, calendar or contacts can read them, and can
pull the body of a specific message on demand even though no body is stored.
None of them can write: all three connectors ship with no actions at all, so
nothing an agent does can send mail, change an event, or edit a contact.

What you are carrying from here is one password, at Apple. It is scoped to this
use and revocable there at any time, and it is the only iCloud credential the
vault holds. Remove it at Apple and Postern stops reading — the three
connections fail their next sync and ask to be reconnected — while nothing else
of yours moves: your account, and every other app-specific password on it, are
untouched. Changing your Apple Account password does the same thing without
asking you. Either way, what Postern has already cached keeps answering. Pulling
the credential in the Console is the deliberate version: it darkens all three
connections at once and deletes the sealed password from the vault, and the rows
already cached stay behind as read-only history until you remove them
separately.
