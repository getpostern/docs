title: "Connect Apple Health"
description: "Your iPhone pushes to an endpoint the Console mints for it. Nothing is pulled, and Postern keeps what your phone sends from the day it starts."
---

<Info>
  **Before you start**

  - **An iPhone, and the Health Auto Export app** — from the App Store. Its REST API automations
    may need the app's paid tier.
  - **About ten minutes**, plus however long your first export takes to send.
  - **Two values, one of them shown once** — the Console mints an ingest path and a device secret.
    Open your password manager before you start.
  - **A network route from your phone to the box.** Everything Postern publishes binds loopback, so
    a phone cannot reach it at all on its own — this is the one source that usually needs
    [remote access](/start/remote-access) set up first. The private tier is enough: Tailscale on the
    phone and on the box, and `tailscale serve` in front of the ingest path. Nothing has to be
    public.
  - **Postern installed and running**, with the Console in front of you.
  - **Push, not pull.** Your phone's automation decides when data moves. Postern cannot fetch
    anything from Apple Health, and this connection has no Sync now.
  - **What lands here is what your phone sends from now on** — a faithful cache of a push, not a
    backup of your Health store. There is no history to backfill.
</Info>

<Steps>

<Step title="Mint the ingest endpoint in the Console" titleSize="h2" id="mint-endpoint">

Console → Add a source → Apple Health. The card says what the button will do before you press it:
Postern mints a private ingest endpoint and a one-time secret for this device, and nothing is
created until you ask.

<Frame caption="The state before the click. Nothing exists yet — the path and the secret appear only after you press the button.">
  <img src="/images/apple-health-endpoint.png" alt="The Console's Apple Health connect card, headed YOUR INGEST ENDPOINT, reading: Postern mints a private ingest endpoint and a one-time secret for this device. Nothing is created until you ask. Beneath it, one poppy button labelled Create your ingest endpoint." />
</Frame>

<Warning>
  The device secret is shown once. Postern keeps only its SHA-256 hash, so nothing on the box can
  produce it again — not the connection screen, not the log, not the database. If you lose it, the
  only way forward is **Rotate secret** on the connection, which mints a fresh one and stops every
  device still sending the old one. Have somewhere to put it before you press the button.
</Warning>

Press **Create your ingest endpoint**. The card returns two values — **Ingest path**, which is
`/api/ingest/<connection-id>`, and **Device secret — shown once** — and then sits at *waiting for
the first push*.

The path is not a URL. Postern is never told your remote-access host: the Console runs on loopback
and has no way to know what address stands in front of it. So you compose the URL yourself — your
tailnet address for the ingest port, then the path exactly as the Console printed it. The Console
shows this same example beside the path, with its own placeholder host.

```text
https://gateway.example-tailnet.ts.net:8443/api/ingest/<connection-id>
```

Whatever address you put in front of it will work: the ingest route is the one route Postern serves
without a host check, deliberately, because it is the only one designed to be reached from off the
box. Its per-connection secret is what stands in for that check.

Copy the composed URL and the secret. Both get pasted into your phone in the two automations below.

<Note>
  Coming back to this screen once a connection exists shows *A device is already pushing Health data
  here. Create another endpoint only if you're adding a second device*, and makes you tick *Yes —
  create a second ingest endpoint* before the button works. Take it literally: a second endpoint is
  a second connection for a second device, not a repair. To put a working secret back on the phone
  you already have, open the existing connection and use **Rotate secret**.
</Note>

</Step>

<Step title="Install Health Auto Export on the iPhone" titleSize="h2" id="install-app">

On the iPhone, install **Health Auto Export** from the App Store. Its REST API automations may need
the app's paid tier — that is the app's own paywall, not Postern's. Once it opens it carries an
**Automations** tab, which is where the next two steps happen.

<Note>
  If you would rather not pay for the app, the Console's own Apple Health card carries the request
  under *The JSON body Postern expects*, with a **Copy the template** button and the rules that
  decide whether a payload lands: each `metrics[].name` becomes a metric type, the metric named
  `sleep_analysis` becomes a sleep session, every entry under `workouts[]` needs its own `id`, and
  dates are `yyyy-MM-dd HH:mm:ss ±hhmm` rather than ISO — a point Postern cannot date is skipped
  rather than stored wrong. Anything that can POST that body with the secret header works, and the
  card spells out an iPhone Shortcut built from a *Get Contents of URL* step. This page documents
  the app because that is the path that runs in production.
</Note>

</Step>

<Step title="Build the metrics automation" titleSize="h2" id="metrics-automation">

Health Auto Export → Automations → new. These are the settings the production gateway's own phone
runs with, recovered from its exported automation — set every field to match. The automation is
saved once it appears in the automations list; nothing reaches Postern until it runs.

```text
Export Data Type Health Metrics
Destination REST API
URL                   the full URL you composed in step 1
Headers Authorization: Bearer <your secret>
        (the connect card's X-PCI-Webhook-Secret: <your secret> also works)
Export Format JSON · Export Version v2
Export File Length day · Batch Requests OFF
Export Aggregation Default · Export Period Default
Aggregate Data ON · Aggregate Sleep ON
Include health metrics ON · workouts OFF
Include cycle tracking ON · medications ON
                      · ECG OFF · heart-rate notifications OFF
                      · state of mind OFF · symptoms OFF
```

For the metric selection: production selects essentially the full list. Start with everything and
prune later, since each day's file is small.

<Warning>
  An exported Health Auto Export automation carries your live ingest URL and secret in its JSON, in
  plain text — that is how the settings above were recovered in the first place. Sharing one as a
  sample hands someone a working key to your health data, and a shared secret cannot be unshared.
  Scrub the URL and the headers before a config leaves your phone, or rotate the secret afterwards.
</Warning>

<Note>
  Leave **Export File Length** on `day` and **Batch Requests** off. Postern refuses any single push
  over 25 MB with `{"error":"payload too large"}`, and nothing in a refused push lands — a longer
  file length is how you reach that ceiling. A day of metrics stays well under it even with
  everything selected.
</Note>

</Step>

<Step title="Build the workouts automation" titleSize="h2" id="workouts-automation">

In the same place — Health Auto Export → Automations → new — build a second automation, identical
in every field above, with the data type changed:

```text
Export Data Type Workouts
Include health metrics OFF · workouts ON
Include Workout Metadata ON · Include Routes ON
```

Everything else matches the first automation exactly: the same URL, the same header, JSON, v2, file
length day, batch requests off, both aggregation switches on. Saved, you have two automations in the
list — one Health Metrics, one Workouts, both with Destination REST API and both pointed at the same
URL.

Splitting the two by data type keeps each payload small and each failure legible: when something
stops arriving, you can see which half stopped. Note which half carries sleep — the metrics
automation does. Health Auto Export exports sleep as a metric named `sleep_analysis`, and Postern
turns that one metric into sleep sessions.

</Step>

<Step title="Set the schedule, then run one by hand" titleSize="h2" id="first-push">

In Health Auto Export, set each automation's schedule — the production gateway's phone pushes every
hour, and hourly is a good default — then run one automation by hand rather than waiting for it.

Watch the connection at Console → Sources → Apple Health. Until the first push lands it carries the
badge **Awaiting first push**. When the push lands that badge disappears entirely and the line reads
**Last synced** with a time, under a caption reading **Device controlled**. A healthy connection has
no badge here: the timestamp is the signal, and there is no predicted next time because your phone
never promised one.

Run it a second time if you want to watch it again. Health Auto Export's export windows overlap on
purpose, and Postern gives every sample, sleep session and workout a deterministic id, so a re-post
updates the same rows rather than duplicating them.

Keep the schedule inside two days. After 48 hours without a push the connection reads **Stale**,
which here is advice rather than a fault: Postern can never reach out to your phone, so silence is
ambiguous, and it will never call a device **Unreachable**.

<Note>
  Nothing arrives, and nothing on the box explains why. Every rejection at the ingest endpoint
  answers with the same `401` and the same body, `{"error":"unauthorized"}` — a wrong secret, a
  wrong connection id, a paused connection and a connection that never had a secret are
  deliberately indistinguishable from outside, so nobody can probe the endpoint to learn what
  exists. Check in this order: that the URL carries the connection id the Console printed; that the
  header holds the secret exactly; that the connection is not paused, since **Pause receiving**
  stops pushes at the door while cached data keeps serving; and that the phone can reach the box at
  all. A request that never arrives leaves no trace on the gateway, which is what the probes under
  [Serve the two ports to your own devices](/start/remote-access#serve-the-two-ports-to-your-own-devices)
  are for — they separate a routing failure from an auth failure.
</Note>

</Step>

</Steps>

## Confirm it works

Each of these is visible without running anything, and each is a place this goes wrong quietly.

- **Console → Sources**, the Apple Health connection: the **Awaiting first push** badge is gone, the
  line reads **Last synced** with a time, and the caption beneath reads **Device controlled**.
- **The connection's Delivery panel**: **Shared secret** reads as set, with a date, rather than *no
  secret minted yet* — and the button offers **Rotate secret** rather than **Mint secret**.
- **Health Auto Export → Automations**: two automations, one Health Metrics and one Workouts, both
  with Destination REST API and both carrying the same URL.
- **One Apple Health connection in the Console, not two.** A second one means the endpoint was
  minted twice, and only the newest secret is the one your phone is sending.

## What you have now

A health connection fed by your own phone, on your own schedule, with no cloud in between. The
ingest endpoint accepts nothing without its secret: the check runs before the body is read, compares
hashes in constant time, and fails closed. What an agent can see is what your phone has sent since
the day you set this up — sleep, workouts and every metric you selected. Health is read-only here,
so a key granted `health` reads it and there is nothing to act on.

Freshness travels with the answer. The connection's state — awaiting a first push, a bare timestamp,
stale after two days of silence — is computed in one place on the server and served identically to
the Console and to an agent's `describe_context`, so an agent asking about your sleep can see how
current the answer is instead of assuming.

The standing costs are yours. Your phone's schedule is the only schedule, and Postern cannot pull:
when the data looks old, the fix is to run the automation on the phone. The secret lives in that
automation until you rotate it, and rotating it stops every device still using the old one.
**Pause receiving** stops the pushes at the door without touching what has already landed — and a
paused connection refuses a push in exactly the way a wrong secret does.
