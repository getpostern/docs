<Info>
  **Before you start**

  - **A Plaid developer account** — free to create at
    [dashboard.plaid.com](https://dashboard.plaid.com), and the account these keys belong to.
    Postern includes no Plaid access of its own.
  - **Money, per linked bank.** Plaid's Production access is pay-as-you-go: Transactions is billed
    monthly for every bank you keep linked, Balance per request, and Plaid publishes no price list —
    you cannot cost it out in advance. A new US or Canada team is put on the free Trial plan
    instead: real bank data, up to ten banks, no security questionnaire.
  - **Hosted Link — no token to paste, and no redirect URI to register with Plaid.** You sign in to
    your bank on a page Plaid hosts, and the Console picks up the result by polling. That is what
    makes this work on a machine nothing can reach from outside.
  - **US banks.** Postern mints the Link session with the United States as its only country, so
    Link offers US institutions.
  - **SimpleFIN first, Plaid for the gaps.** SimpleFIN is Postern's default finance source: one
    subscription you pay, covering every bank it reaches. Plaid is the coverage upgrade and bills
    per bank, so link Plaid for the banks SimpleFIN cannot. Its guide is at
    [Connect SimpleFIN](/connect/simplefin).
  - **A running Postern with the Console in front of you.** Everything on this page happens at
    Console → Sources → Add a source → Plaid. If Postern runs on a NAS, a home server or anything
    else headless, bring the Console to yourself first:
    [Set up remote access](/start/remote-access).
  - **Your password manager, open before you paste.** The client secret is sealed in the vault the
    moment you save and is never shown again — the field afterwards reads *already sealed — leave
    blank to keep* — and Plaid is the only other place you can read it.
  - **A standing cost.** Bank consent expires about once a year, per bank, and each lapse is a
    reconnect you perform.
</Info>

## Choose your path

Four ways to arrive here. Only the first runs the whole page.

| If… | Then |
|---|---|
| this Console has no Plaid app saved yet | start at [Create your Plaid developer account](#create-your-plaid-developer-account) |
| the app is saved and you are adding another bank | Console → Sources → Add a source → Plaid → **Add a bank**, then [Sign in to your bank in the Plaid window](#sign-in-to-your-bank-in-the-plaid-window) |
| a bank you already linked reads **consent lapsed** | [Reconnect a bank when its consent lapses](#reconnect-a-bank-when-its-consent-lapses) |
| you want to run the flow without a real bank | [Copy your client ID and Production secret](#copy-your-client-id-and-production-secret) carries the Sandbox route and what it costs |

<Steps>

<Step title="Create your Plaid developer account" titleSize="h2" id="plaid-account">

Sign up at [dashboard.plaid.com](https://dashboard.plaid.com). The team you land in decides what
the rest of this page costs you: a new US or Canada team is put on Plaid's free Trial plan — real
bank data, up to ten linked banks, no security questionnaire — and an existing team uses its
Production access, which is paid. If Plaid's Trial page sends you to your dashboard overview
rather than to a plan, your team predates Trial and you are on pay-as-you-go Production. Either
way you end this step signed in to a Plaid team, with no keys copied and nothing connected.

<Warning>
  Trial counts ten banks for the lifetime of the team, and removing a bank does not give the slot
  back. A bank you link only to see whether the flow works is one of the ten, permanently. Plaid's
  Sandbox is where that test belongs — fake institutions, no slot spent — and the next step says
  what reaching it costs.
</Warning>

</Step>

<Step title="Copy your client ID and Production secret" titleSize="h2" id="keys">

In the Plaid dashboard, open the Keys page —
[dashboard.plaid.com/developers/keys](https://dashboard.plaid.com/developers/keys), which the
dashboard reaches through Developers → Keys. Postern's own Plaid screen names the older path,
Team Settings → Keys; both are that one page, and Plaid has moved it before. Copy the `client_id`,
then copy the **Production** secret. You end this step holding two strings, with nothing changed on
either side.

The `client_id` is the same string in every Plaid environment. The secret is not: there is one per
environment, and the only one this Console can use is Production.

<Note>
  The Console saves whatever you paste as a **Production** app — its own footnote on that screen
  says *Console defaults to Production*, and it sends nothing else. A Sandbox secret pasted there
  reaches Plaid's production API, which refuses it: no Link session is minted, no Plaid tab opens,
  and the Console reports the refusal as Plaid's. Reach Sandbox the headless way instead — put
  `PLAID_CLIENT_ID`, `PLAID_SECRET` and `PLAID_ENV=sandbox` in the same `.env` your stack starts
  from and bring the stack up again. A Plaid app saved in the Console wins over all three
  variables, so Sandbox answers only while no app is saved, and clearing a saved app at Console →
  Settings → Provider apps → Clear stops every bank that uses it from refreshing until you
  configure it again. Sandbox institutions take `user_good` / `pass_good` and cost no Trial slot.
</Note>

<Note>
  Banks that use OAuth can refuse an app whose display information is empty, even on Trial, and the
  refusal arrives inside the bank's own sign-in page rather than in Postern — where nothing
  identifies the cause. Fill in your app's display name and logo in the Plaid dashboard before you
  link a bank.
</Note>

</Step>

<Step title="Paste both into the Console" titleSize="h2" id="paste-the-app">

Console → Sources → Add a source → Plaid. Paste the `client_id` into **Client ID**, the Production
secret into **Secret**, and press **Save & add a bank**. The secret is sealed in the vault as it
saves and is never returned to the screen again; Postern then mints a Plaid-hosted Link session,
opens it in a new tab, and the panel in front of you switches to **Link your banks** and starts
polling for the result.

<Step title="Sign in to your bank in the Plaid window" titleSize="h2" id="link-the-bank">

In the new tab, Plaid asks for a phone number before anything else. **Continue without phone
number**, the line under the Continue button, skips it. Then choose your bank, sign in with the
bank's own credentials, and finish. Postern never sees that sign-in: it happens on Plaid's page,
and what returns to the Console is a token for that one bank. On a Trial team, every real bank you
finish here is one of the ten.

Plaid's page may carry a banner saying the app is testing something new. Plaid shows that for apps
without full Production access, so it reflects your own Plaid team rather than Postern, and nothing
in the Console can turn it off. A bank can also need time on Plaid's side before it works with a new
app: most are minutes to hours, and Charles Schwab has taken up to about six weeks.

<Warning>
  Banks you linked at my.plaid.com do not transfer. Plaid reads only the banks linked inside this
  gateway's own Link session, so an account you connected there is invisible here and there is
  nothing to import — you link it again through this window, and on a Trial team it spends one of
  the ten.
</Warning>

If no tab opened at all, a pop-up blocker stopped it: the panel waiting behind it carries a
**Re-open Plaid Link** button that opens the same session.

</Step>

<Step title="Watch the bank land in the Console" titleSize="h2" id="first-bank">

Leave the Console's **Link your banks** panel open. It polls the session every few seconds, and
while it waits it shows *waiting for the first bank*. When you finish at Plaid, the bank appears in
that panel as its own connection, with its name and its health, and the panel's button changes from
**Continue in Sources** to **Done — go to Sources**. Follow it: Sources now carries a Plaid group
with that bank nested beneath it, and a bank still drawing its first pages reads **Syncing…**.

<Frame caption="This panel is the Console, not Plaid. Re-open Plaid Link reopens the same session; Continue in Sources leaves it running.">

    alt="The Console's Link your banks panel: the line Plaid Link opened in a new tab — choose your bank(s) and authorise there, then a waiting indicator reading waiting for the first bank, a Re-open Plaid Link button and a poppy Continue in Sources button."
  />
</Frame>

<Note>
  One Connect session links one bank. The panel says *bank(s)* because a multi-bank Link session
  needs a Plaid user token that Plaid issues only to apps enrolled in products this gateway does not
  use, so the session Postern mints carries a single bank. For the next bank, go back to Console →
  Sources → Add a source → Plaid and press **Add a bank** — the client ID is already filled and the
  secret stays sealed, so leave that field blank.
</Note>

<Note>
  If you close Plaid's tab without finishing, the session ends with nothing in it: the panel goes on
  showing *waiting for the first bank* while its button changes to **Done — go to Sources**, and no
  bank row ever appears. Nothing was linked and no slot was spent. The link Postern mints is good
  for three hours in any case. Start again from Add a source → Plaid.
</Note>

</Step>

<Step title="Reconnect a bank when its consent lapses" titleSize="h2" id="reconnect">

Bank consent expires about once a year — Capital One, Fidelity and Bank of America all enforce it —
and a bank whose consent has lapsed goes quiet rather than loud: Plaid returns empty pages, which
reads exactly like a week with no transactions. Postern polls each bank's standing separately for
that reason, and surfaces it. At Console → Sources, such a bank carries **consent lapsed** where its
schedule usually sits, and its action reads **Reconnect →** rather than **Manage →**. Open the
connection and press **Reconnect at Plaid**. That runs Plaid's hosted update flow against the same
bank — no re-paste, no fresh link — and on success the connection keeps its identity, resumes from
the cursor it stopped at, and backfills what it missed. The alarm clears and the schedule returns
to the row.

<Note>
  Two states the update flow cannot repair. A connection Postern marks **dead** has lost the Item
  behind it, so there is nothing to update — link the bank again from Add a source → Plaid, which
  creates a new connection and, on a Trial team, spends a slot. A connection you disconnected has no
  credential left in the vault at all, and the Console says so rather than offering a reconnect that
  cannot work.
</Note>

</Step>

</Steps>

## Confirm it works

What this proves, in order: the app is saved, the bank is linked, it is on a schedule, its empty
first read is expected, and an agent can see it.

- Console → Sources carries a **Plaid** group, and beneath it one row for each linked bank, with an
  account count and a schedule. The group's own line says how many banks feed it.
- The bank's schedule reads a cadence, not a warning. Plaid banks share one cadence — six hours
  unless you change it, and never faster than fifteen minutes.
- A bank that landed a moment ago reads **Syncing…**. Plaid's first sync commonly returns nothing
  and the history arrives over the syncs that follow, so an empty first read is the expected shape
  rather than a failure.
- Console → Settings → Provider apps: **Plaid** reads configured.
- If the same account also arrives through SimpleFIN, the bank's row carries *also arrives via
  SimpleFIN — served as one*. Agents are served one merged row; both provider rows stay intact
  underneath and can still be read individually.
- Ask an agent whose key was granted **finance** to describe its context. The Plaid connection is
  listed with its own health, and with the date its consent expires once Plaid has published one.
  Granting is done at Console → Agents & keys — see [Connect an agent](/start/connect-an-agent).

## What you have now

One connection for each bank you linked, read-only in both directions that matter: finance carries
no actions, so no agent can move money, and Postern never held the sign-in that would let it.
Accounts, transactions and holdings are cached locally and polled on a schedule Plaid banks share —
six hours by default, fifteen minutes at the fastest — and any agent whose key opens **finance**
answers from that cache. The history window was set when the bank was linked: Postern asks Plaid for
up to two years, and that window is fixed for the life of the link — widening it means linking the
bank again. Where a bank also arrives through SimpleFIN, the two are reconciled into one served row
and both originals stay readable underneath.

What you are carrying from here. Plaid bills Production per linked bank, monthly, for as long as
the bank stays linked; a Trial team instead has ten bank slots for its lifetime, and removing a bank
does not return one. Balances an agent reads from the cache cost nothing extra, and an explicit live
balance drill goes to a Plaid endpoint billed per call. Bank consent expires about once a year, per
bank, and each lapse is a reconnect you perform — Postern polls for the lapse and shows it, but
cannot renew it for you. Disconnecting a bank tells Plaid to revoke that link at its end — Plaid's
`/item/remove` — which is also what stops its billing; your other Plaid banks are untouched. That
revocation is one-way: a disconnected or erased bank cannot be reconnected, only linked again from
the start, on a fresh slot.
