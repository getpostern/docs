title: "Connect Google and Gmail"
description: "Calendar and contacts through your own Google Cloud app; Gmail separately over IMAP. The two parts are independent — do either one, or both."
---

<Info>
  **Before you start**

  - **Your own Google Cloud app, and one app password.** Both are yours, both are created in this
    session, and both stay on your own machine — Postern seals them locally and sends them only to
    Google.
  - **About 20 minutes** — roughly five for Gmail, fifteen for calendar and contacts.
  - **Weekly re-consent until you publish to production.** While your Cloud app's publishing status
    is Testing, Google expires the app's access every 7 days. The last step on this page ends that.
  - **2-Step Verification on your Google account.** Part A cannot start without it, and turning it
    on changes your Google account, not Postern.
  - **Two secrets, each shown once, each in a dialog you can close by accident** — a sixteen-character
    app password and a client secret. Open your password manager before you start.
  - **Postern installed, and a browser that reaches the Console at `http://localhost:8787`.** Work on
    the machine Postern runs on, or bring the Console to yourself over SSH first
    ([Set up remote access](/start/remote-access#reach-the-console-over-ssh-when-it-runs-on-another-box)). Google sends the sign-in back to
    `localhost:8787`, so that address has to be live in the browser you are sitting at.
</Info>

## Choose your path

Gmail does not go through the OAuth app, and that is deliberate. Reading mail through Google's
Gmail API means restricted scopes, and restricted scopes mean an annual third-party CASA security
assessment for every self-hosted deployment. So mail comes in over IMAP with an app password
instead: the Cloud app you register below asks for no Gmail scope, ever, and a test in the product
asserts that no Gmail scope can appear in the sign-in URL. The two parts are independent — either
one works on its own, in either order.

| If you want… | Then |
|---|---|
| mail | [Part A](#turn-on-2-step-verification) — 2-Step Verification, then one app password over IMAP. About five minutes. |
| calendar and contacts | [Part B](#enable-the-two-apis-in-one-project) — one Google Cloud project, one OAuth client, one consent. About fifteen minutes. |
| all three | [Part A](#turn-on-2-step-verification), then [Part B](#enable-the-two-apis-in-one-project). This page does them in that order, but neither depends on the other. |
| a Google Workspace account | Part B's Audience page also offers **Internal**, which is offered only to Workspace organisations. Every step below is written for **External**, including [the Test-users step](#configure-the-consent-screen-and-add-yourself), which an Internal app does not use. |

<Steps>

<Step title="Turn on 2-Step Verification" titleSize="h2" id="two-step-verification">

At [myaccount.google.com](https://myaccount.google.com) → Security → 2-Step Verification, turn it
on. The App passwords page does not exist until you do: no amount of searching the Security page
surfaces it beforehand, because Google does not offer app passwords to an account without 2-Step
Verification. Once it is on, the address in the next step resolves.

</Step>

<Step title="Create the app password and paste it with your address" titleSize="h2" id="app-password">

Go straight to the address below rather than hunting for it — the Security page does not link it.
Name the password `Postern`, and Google shows you sixteen characters in four groups.

```text
myaccount.google.com/apppasswords
```

Behind it, the list of app passwords holds one named postern, and an App name field for creating another." />
</Frame>

Then, in the Console, go to Add a source → Gmail, and fill in both fields: your full Gmail address
and those sixteen characters. The address is the IMAP login and the Console will not accept the
password without it. Save, and the screen switches to its connected state — the panel reads that
Gmail is already connected, and the button changes to `Reconnect Gmail`. The connection appears
under Console → Sources as **mail**. Mail bodies are never stored: only envelopes are cached, and
content is fetched on demand.

The numbered steps on the left are from an earlier Console build, which sends you to the Security page; use the address above.">
  <img src="/images/google-apppasswords.png" alt="The Console's Gmail connect screen: a Gmail address field showing you@gmail.com, an App password field showing xxxx xxxx xxxx xxxx, and a footer reading envelopes only, bodies are fetched on demand, never stored. A note says Postern never sees your Google password — only this scoped, revocable app-password, read-only over IMAP, sealed in the local vault." />
</Frame>

<Note>
  The Console refuses the paste with `A Gmail app password is 16 characters — check you copied all
  of it (spaces are fine, they're ignored).` It counts after removing whitespace, and it strips
  whitespace before sealing, so the four groups can go in exactly as Google prints them — spaces,
  no spaces, either. A count that is not sixteen means part of it did not come across. And if the
  dialog closes before you copy anything, delete that entry on the App passwords page and create
  another; there is no way to re-read one, and nothing else is affected.
</Note>

</Step>

<Step title="Enable the two APIs in one project" titleSize="h2" id="enable-apis">

Everything in Part B happens inside one Google Cloud project. At
[console.cloud.google.com](https://console.cloud.google.com), open the project picker at the top of
the page → New project, and stay in that project from here on — the client you create later belongs
to it, and so do the API switches you are about to throw.

In that project, go to APIs & Services → Library, search for `Google Calendar API` and enable it,
then search for `People API` and enable it. Each one's page then reads **Enabled** under Status.

<Warning>
  Searching the Library for `contacts` surfaces the legacy **Contacts API** first, and enabling it
  does nothing for Postern. Contacts come through the **People API** — service name
  `people.googleapis.com`, against the legacy one's `contacts.googleapis.com`, which is the check
  that settles it. Google's own product page for the legacy API says it is no longer being
  developed and to migrate to the People API. Getting this wrong costs you an afternoon rather than
  an error message: a 403 on calendar or contacts right after a successful sign-in means the APIs
  are not enabled in the project that owns your client — the most common post-consent failure, and
  the error will not say so. Enable the right two in that project's Library, give Google a minute or
  two to start honouring it, then reconnect from the Console.
</Warning>

<Frame caption="The decoy, in Google's own words: no longer being developed, migrate to the People API. Service name contacts.googleapis.com. If your screen reads like this, you are enabling the wrong one.">
  <img src="/images/google-contacts-decoy.png" alt="The Google Cloud product page for the Contacts API. Additional details list the last product update as 2022 and the service name as contacts.googleapis.com." />
</Frame>

</Step>

<Step title="Configure the consent screen and add yourself" titleSize="h2" id="consent-screen">

The consent screen comes before the client: the Clients page will not mint one until a consent
screen exists. In the same project, open Google Auth Platform → Audience — that is what the modern
console calls the OAuth consent screen, and the older name still appears in Google's own
documentation and in Postern's Console. Set the audience to **External**, fill in the app name and
the contact emails, and then, under Test users, choose **Add users** and add your own Google
address.

```text
console.cloud.google.com → Google Auth Platform → Audience → Test users → Add users
```

The page then reads Publishing status **Testing**, User type **External**, and shows your address
under Test users, with the user-cap line counting one test user.

<Note>
  Skip the Test users step and Google refuses your own consent with `OAuth access is restricted to
  the test users listed on your OAuth consent screen.` While the app's publishing status is
  Testing, only listed accounts may consent, and a fresh app starts with nobody listed — not even
  the account that created it. Add the address and start the sign-in again from the Console; nothing
  is lost.
</Note>

Clients, in the left nav, is where the next step goes. Nothing on this page asks you to start the free trial the top strip offers.">

</Step>

<Step title="Create the OAuth client, type Web application" titleSize="h2" id="oauth-client">

In the same project, go to Google Auth Platform → Clients → Create client, and choose application
type **Web application**. Postern's own Google screen names the same page by its other name, APIs &
Services → Credentials; both routes land on the Clients page, and Google has been renaming this
area, so expect either.

Under **Authorized redirect URIs** — not the JavaScript origins list above it — add the URI your
own Console prints on its Google screen, using the copy control beside it. Do not type it, and do
not copy it from this page. Google matches that string exactly, and your Console reports the exact
literal your gateway will send: the gateway composes it from its own port rather than from
whatever address you reached the Console at, so the Console is the only copy that stays true if
you ever move the port. On a stock install it reads:

```text
http://localhost:8787/api/oauth/google/callback
```

<Warning>
  Google shows the client secret once, in the dialog that appears when the client is created. Copy
  the client ID and the client secret out of that dialog before you close it. A lost secret cannot
  be revealed again, and the only way back is to delete the client and create another — which
  changes the client ID as well, so anything already holding the old one has to be updated.
</Warning>

<Note>
  That redirect address is where Google sends **your browser** at the end of the sign-in, so
  `http://localhost:8787` has to answer in the browser you run the sign-in from. On the machine
  Postern runs on, it does. From a laptop, with Postern on a NAS or a home server, it only does
  while an SSH tunnel is up — otherwise the consent completes at Google and the return trip lands on
  nothing, and no connection is created. Bring the Console to yourself first:
  [Set up remote access](/start/remote-access#reach-the-console-over-ssh-when-it-runs-on-another-box). Reaching the Console at some other
  address does not change the redirect the gateway sends: that string is pinned server-side, not
  derived from how you got there.
</Note>

</Step>

<Step title="Paste both into the Console and sign in" titleSize="h2" id="sign-in">

In the Console, go to Add a source → Google, put the client ID and the client secret into the two
fields, and choose **Save & sign in with Google**. Sign in as the account you listed under Test
users. Google is a confidential client here: it needs both halves, and the Console will not start
the sign-in with only one — it answers `Google is a confidential client — paste the client secret
too, or the sign-in will be refused.` The secret is sealed in Postern's local vault, and is sent
only to Google, only to refresh access.

On the right, a Client ID field showing an apps.googleusercontent.com placeholder, a Client secret field showing a GOCSPX- placeholder, and a footer listing calendar and contacts from this one consent, noting that Gmail is a separate IMAP connection." />

<Note>
  Any account not on the Test users list is refused, whichever Google account your browser happens
  to be signed into. When Google refuses, the Console puts you back on this same Google screen with
  Google's own error code printed beside the form — that screen is both the report and the repair,
  because the registration steps and the two fields are right there.
</Note>

</Step>

<Step title="Continue past the warning, and tick every box" titleSize="h2" id="grant-scopes">

Two Google screens follow, and both read like failures.

The first says **Google hasn't verified this app**. That is what a Testing-status app looks like to
its own author. **Continue** is the plain link; the prominent blue **Back to safety** button aborts
the sign-in.

<Frame caption="Continue is the plain text link, to the left of the blue button. The blue button is Back to safety, and it aborts.">
  <img src="/images/google-unverified.png" alt="Google's interstitial reading: Google hasn't verified this app. You've been given access to an app that's currently being tested. You should only continue if you know the developer that invited you. At the bottom right, Continue as a plain text link, and a prominent blue Back to safety button beside it." />
</Frame>

The second is the scopes screen, headed **Select what Postern can access**. Postern asks for exactly
two, and both are read-only — `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.readonly` and
`https://www.googleapis.com/auth/contacts.readonly`, which Google renders as *See and download any
calendar that you can access using your Google Calendar* and *See and download your contacts*. No
Gmail scope appears, on this screen or anywhere else.

<Warning>
  The checkboxes on that screen start **empty**, and Google lets you continue with none of them
  ticked. That consent succeeds — no error, no warning — and grants nothing: Postern stores the
  credential and creates no connection, so the source looks half-connected and answers nothing.
  Tick **Select all** before you approve.
</Warning>

Approve, and Google returns you to the Console's Sources screen. The one consent becomes two
connections, calendar and contacts, because Postern creates one connection per sector the consent
actually covered.

<Note>
  The sign-in has ten minutes to complete, once, from the moment you press Save & sign in with
  Google — the gateway holds the pending sign-in in memory for that long and consumes it on first
  use. Wander off mid-flow, restart the gateway, or reuse a stale tab, and the return trip is
  rejected. Nothing is lost: start again from the Console's Google screen.
</Note>

</Step>

<Step title="Publish the app when the weekly re-consent gets old" titleSize="h2" id="publish">

While the publishing status stays Testing, Google expires the refresh token every 7 days and
calendar and contacts stop until you consent again. When that gets old, go back to Google Auth
Platform → Audience — the same page where you added Test users — and choose **Publish app**. The
status changes from Testing to In production. For a single-user app requesting these two scopes it
is a form rather than a review.

Publishing is optional. A weekly re-consent is a working setup rather than a broken one — this step
is how you stop paying for it.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Confirm it works

Three connections in all, and the four registration facts that decide whether they keep working.

- Console → Sources lists three connections: **mail** from Gmail, and **calendar** and **contacts**
  from Google.
- The Console's Gmail and Google screens both read that the source is already connected, and their
  buttons read `Reconnect Gmail` and `Reconnect Google`.
- In the project that owns your client, both APIs read **Enabled** — and the contacts one is the
  People API, `people.googleapis.com`, not `contacts.googleapis.com`.
- Google Auth Platform → Audience lists your own address under Test users.
- Google Auth Platform → Clients holds a **Web application** client whose **Authorized redirect
  URIs** — not its JavaScript origins — contains the exact string the Console's Google screen
  prints.
- Both the client ID and the client secret are in the Console's Google screen. Google is a
  confidential client; one without the other fails the token exchange.

## What you have now

Three connections: Gmail over IMAP from Part A, calendar and contacts from Part B. Any agent granted
those sectors answers from them after the first sync. Mail is envelopes in the cache and bodies
fetched on demand, never stored; calendar and contacts are read-only twice over — the two scopes
Postern requests are read-only, and the connector behind them declares no actions and refuses to run
one.

What you are carrying from here. While the Cloud app's publishing status stays Testing, Google
expires Part B's access every 7 days and you re-consent weekly; publishing the app ends that, and
the Publish app button is on the Audience page you already know. The app password and the OAuth
grant are both revocable at Google, from your Google Account rather than from Postern, and either
one stops its own connections without touching the other. Connecting a source again replaces the
stored credential rather than adding a second one — the Console makes you tick a box saying so
first — and a fresh consent supersedes an earlier revocation. The client secret sits sealed in
Postern's vault on your own machine, and leaves it only to ask Google for fresh access.
