<Info>
  **Before you start**

  - **A ChatGPT paid plan with Developer mode available.** Developer mode is what allows a custom
    MCP server at all, and step 3 is where you turn it on.
  - **A public HTTPS address on port 443, already published.** ChatGPT connects from OpenAI's own
    cloud. It can never reach `localhost`, it can never reach your tailnet, and it egresses on port
    443 only. Postern publishes nothing by default, so this is a job you do first:
    [Set up remote access](/start/remote-access) is the whole of it — *Tailscale + optional Funnel ·
    about 20 minutes, once · off by default*.
  - **About ten minutes**, once that address is up.
  - **An agent key with an expiry on it, and somewhere safe to keep it.** The key is shown once, and
    ChatGPT asks for it at the **end** of the sign-in, in a popup, in another application, after you
    have copied something else over it. Open your password manager before you start. A key that
    never expires is refused by the bridge.
  - **Postern installed and running, with the Console in front of you.** Everything on this side
    happens at Console → Agents & keys. If Postern runs on a NAS, a home server or anything else
    headless, bring the Console to yourself over SSH first:
    [Set up remote access](/start/remote-access).
  - **Witnessed end to end on 2026-08-01.** Every ChatGPT label quoted below is what was on screen
    that day. OpenAI renames these; where this page and your screen disagree, your screen is newer.
</Info>

## Choose your path

Where you start depends on whether you already have a key ChatGPT can use.

| If… | Then |
|---|---|
| You have no agent key in hand, or the only one you have never expires | Mint one — [step 1](#mint-a-key-and-save-it-before-you-go-on) |
| You are standing on the reveal screen from a key you have this minute minted, and the key is already saved somewhere | Start at [step 2](#copy-the-endpoint-from-the-console) |

<Steps>

<Step title="Mint a key and save it before you go on" titleSize="h2" id="mint-a-key">

Console → Agents & keys → **Mint a key**. Name it: the name is what the ledger prints under **Who**,
and `chatgpt` reads well there. Then tick the sectors ChatGPT may reach. Granting a sector grants
**read and act** across every provider in it — there is nothing narrower to grant, and a key with no
sectors at all still mints, still connects, and is denied on every read.

Leave **Expires** on one of the day chips. The chips are **Never**, **30 days**, **60 days** and
**90 days**; once this gateway has an address saved, the form starts on **90 days** and prints
**This gate is published. A key you paste into a hosted client should expire.** underneath. A key
that never expires is not merely unwise here — the bridge refuses it at the sign-in, and you find
out four steps from now.

<Warning>
  The key is shown once and Postern keeps only a hash of it, so there is nothing to re-reveal and
  the only remedy is minting again. The very next step then puts your endpoint on the clipboard,
  over the key. Witnessed 2026-08-01: the endpoint went into the key box at the end of the flow and
  the sign-in answered `That agent key was not accepted. Check the key in the Console and try again.`
  Put the key in your password manager before you touch anything else.
</Warning>

Press **Mint the key**. The reveal screen opens, headed with the agent's name and the words
**is ready**, and the key sits under **Agent key — shown once** with a **Copy** button beside it.

</Step>

<Step title="Copy the endpoint from the Console" titleSize="h2" id="copy-the-endpoint">

From the reveal screen you are standing on, follow
**Connect a hosted client (claude.ai, ChatGPT) →**. From anywhere else: Console → Agents & keys →
**Set up the bridge →**. Both open the same page, headed **Connect claude.ai or ChatGPT**.

When your address is one a hosted client can use, that page prints it under **Paste this into
claude.ai or ChatGPT** with a **Copy** button beside it. Take it from there rather than from this
page. What ChatGPT needs is your published origin **plus `/mcp`** — the address Postern saves in
Settings is the bare origin, and the Console appends that suffix itself, in one place, so the two
can never drift. The shape it prints is:

```text
https://gateway.example-tailnet.ts.net/mcp
```

The endpoint block on the reveal screen, above the link you followed, prints this gateway's **local**
address instead. ChatGPT can never reach that one.

<Note>
  The page offers no **Copy** button, and prints a pattern such as
  `https://your-mac.tailnet.ts.net/mcp` where your address should be. The sentence beside it says
  which of five things is true, and they have different fixes. `Bridge is off — agents can only
  reach this gate with a pasted key` means nothing has ever been published —
  [set up remote access](/start/remote-access). `Bridge is off — an address is saved but not
  published yet` means the address is saved and the gateway has not restarted into it; restart it.
  `Bridge is off — this gateway couldn't read its own exposure setting` sends you to Settings →
  Remote access, which says why. `This gateway didn’t answer with its bridge status` is a fact about
  the Console's own request and says nothing about the box; reload. And where an address is saved
  that a cloud client cannot use, the page names the reason — a loopback address, plain `http`, or a
  port that is not 443: `Hosted clients egress on port 443 only; on any other port the connection is
  reset with nothing you'll see.`
</Note>

</Step>

<Step title="Turn on Developer mode in ChatGPT" titleSize="h2" id="developer-mode">

In ChatGPT: Settings → **Plugins** → the **Developer mode** row, at the bottom of that list. It
lands you in **Security and login**, wearing an **ELEVATED RISK** badge. The badge is expected. It
is the switch that allows a custom MCP server at all, and a custom MCP server is a program you have
pointed ChatGPT at rather than one OpenAI reviewed. Turn it on, and the row reads on from then.

<Note>
  Nothing in ChatGPT's menus is called **Connectors**, and you will go looking for it. The settings
  rail says **Plugins**, ChatGPT's own banner says connectors are now called **Apps**, and the URLs
  still say Connectors. Three names, one thing. Go by what the rail says.
</Note>

</Step>

<Step title="Create the connector and point it at your address" titleSize="h2" id="create-the-connector">

At chatgpt.com/plugins, press the **+** button, top right. In the **New Plugin** form: **Name**
`Postern`; **Server URL**, the endpoint you copied in step 2; **Authentication**, **OAuth**. Under
the red warning, tick **I understand and want to continue** — it is alarming by design, and what it
is telling you is true: you are pointing ChatGPT at a server of your own rather than one OpenAI
vouches for. Press **Create**, and a sign-in popup opens on your own hostname.

<Note>
  ChatGPT answers that it could not reach your server, and nothing at all appears in your logs.
  Nothing arrived: a Funnel on any port but 443 is reset before the request leaves OpenAI's network,
  so there is no request to log. Fix the address first. Then wait before you retry — a hosted client
  remembers a failed check for a few minutes, and the Console says so in its own words: **If
  claude.ai or ChatGPT says it can't connect, fix the address, wait five minutes, and try once.**
  Re-submitting the form immediately reads the same cached failure back to you.
</Note>

</Step>

<Step title="Approve the sign-in on your own machine" titleSize="h2" id="approve-the-sign-in">

The popup is served by your own Postern, on your own hostname, and it is plain and unstyled — that
is what it looks like today, not a failure. It is headed **Authorize agent access**, then names the
client that asked — **ChatGPT** *is asking to connect to your gateway.* — then states where
approving sends you: *Approving sends a sign-in code to* **chatgpt.com/connector/oauth**. *If you
did not start this from that app, close this page.*

<Warning>
  Read that destination line before you approve. The bridge accepts a client registration from
  anyone who can reach your published address — that is what the OAuth standard requires of it, not
  a choice Postern made — so the client name and the destination on that page are the only two
  things telling you this request came from the application you started it from. Approving sends a
  one-time sign-in code to whatever that destination is. If either line is not ChatGPT's, close the
  page.
</Warning>

Paste the key from step 1 into **Paste an agent key from the Console**, and press **Approve**. The
popup closes and ChatGPT reads **✓ Postern is installed.** Directly after installing it also reads
**No app actions available yet.** — that is what a newly installed connector says, not a broken
connection.

<Note>
  Your browser's password manager pops over the key field, offering to save or replace a login.
  Dismiss it. It can cover the **Approve** button underneath, and an agent key does not belong in a
  password entry — it is a bearer token for your own machine, not a login for ChatGPT.
</Note>

<Note>
  The page answers **This agent key never expires. Keys used with a cloud client must have an expiry
  — mint or rotate a key with a 30/90/365-day expiry in the Console, then try again.** The key
  itself is good; its lifetime is not, and the bridge is the only place that rule is enforced, which
  is why a key minted before this gateway was published arrives here rather than failing earlier.
  Rotate that agent's key in the Console with an expiry on it — the chips on offer are **Never**,
  **30 days**, **60 days** and **90 days** — and run this step again with the new key. Rotating
  keeps the agent's name and its grants, hands you a fresh secret once, and deletes the old key's
  derived tokens in the same act.
</Note>

</Step>

<Step title="Ask ChatGPT something, then read the ledger" titleSize="h2" id="ask-and-check">

In a chat, open the composer's **+** menu → **Postern**. A `[P] Postern` chip lands in the composer
and you type after it. ChatGPT shows its own humanised progress — **Describing the Current Context**
where Postern was asked for `describe_context` — never the raw tool name, and it asks no permission
per call: the sectors you ticked in step 1 are the whole of the ceremony, once and in advance. The
**Sources** rail gaining a **Postern** entry is the sign that a call landed.

<Warning>
  ChatGPT can narrate work it never did. Witnessed 2026-08-01: fifty seconds of **Calculating your
  weekly Postern total…**, then an answer that said it had no data, and a ledger in which no call
  had ever arrived. Your ledger is the only witness there is — when an answer smells empty, open
  Console → The ledger before you change anything else. And that failure carries its own wrong
  remedy: the link it offers points at chatgpt.com/apps, the public directory, where a connector you
  created yourself can never appear. Yours is on the plugins page, under **Personal → Created by
  me**.
</Warning>

</Step>

</Steps>

## Confirm it works

The first three are ChatGPT telling you about itself. The last two are the only ones that prove a
call reached your machine.

- ChatGPT's plugins page lists **Postern** under **Personal → Created by me**.
- In a chat, the composer's **+** menu offers **Postern**, and picking it drops a `[P] Postern` chip
  into the composer.
- The **Sources** rail carries a **Postern** entry after an answer.
- Console → The ledger holds a row with your agent's own name under **Who** and the tool it called
  under **Tool**. Leave the sector filter alone while you look: **One sector at a time also leaves
  out every call that belonged to no sector — an agent asking what the gate holds, or a handshake
  the gate refused.**
- An empty answer **with** a ledger row beside it means the key is connected, the grant is working,
  and this gateway has no sources yet. An empty answer with **no** row means nothing arrived.

## What you have now

ChatGPT holds a rotating token minted by your own machine, never the key itself. The bridge issues a
short-lived access token and a refresh token beside it, and both resolve to the same grant row your
key resolves to — so ChatGPT sees exactly the sectors you ticked in step 1, and appears in the
ledger under exactly the name you gave it. A chain of refreshes can never outlive the key's own
expiry.

Revoke or rotate that key in the Console and every token derived from it stops working. The grant
row is the living authority and is read on every call, so a revoked key is refused at the next
request; rotating goes further and deletes that agent's bridge tokens outright, in the same
transaction that replaces the key.

Three standing costs come with this. The key you pasted now lives on OpenAI's infrastructure and
dies on the date you chose — when it does, ChatGPT stops being answered, and a replacement is this
page again from step 1. The address stays published until you stop publishing it, and stopping is
what turns the sign-in off: **Stop publishing and the sign-in disappears — nothing else about this
gate changes.** And ChatGPT asks no permission per call, so the sector list you ticked is the entire
boundary between it and your data for as long as that key lives.

Reads are answered from Postern's cache, on your own machine. A wait you feel in ChatGPT is the
model thinking; it is not the gateway you are waiting for.
