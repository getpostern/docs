<Info>
  **Before you start**

  - **A SimpleFIN Bridge subscription** — about $1.50 a month, paid by you, at
    [bridge.simplefin.org](https://bridge.simplefin.org). The US has no free universal bank API, and
    that subscription is the honest cost of coverage.
  - **About five minutes** in Postern, once your banks are linked at the Bridge.
  - **One token to paste** — a setup token, which is a long base64 string, or the access URL it
    converts into. Nothing to install, no developer account, no OAuth app to register.
  - **Your banks are linked at the Bridge, not here.** Every bank login lives at SimpleFIN. Postern
    never sees one, and which banks this connection carries is decided over there.
  - **The setup token can be claimed once.** Postern spends it during the paste. A token you have
    already claimed somewhere else arrives here spent.
  - **A Console you can reach.** Everything on the Postern side happens in the Console, which binds
    loopback and answers only on the machine Postern runs on. If that machine is a NAS, a home server
    or anything else headless, bring the Console to yourself first —
    [Set up remote access](/start/remote-access) is the SSH tunnel, and it publishes nothing.
</Info>

## Choose your path

What you paste depends on what you are holding, and the same field in the Console takes either.

| If… | Then |
|---|---|
| you have not linked any banks yet | start at [Link your banks at the Bridge](#link-your-banks-at-the-bridge) |
| you hold a setup token — a long base64 string | skip to [Paste the token into the Console](#paste-the-token-into-the-console) |
| you hold the access URL itself — it begins `https://` and carries a token before an `@` | [paste that instead](#paste-the-token-into-the-console); nothing is claimed, and it is sealed as it stands |
| SimpleFIN is already connected here | [pasting again replaces the stored credential](#paste-the-token-into-the-console) rather than adding a second connection |

<Steps>

<Step title="Link your banks at the Bridge" titleSize="h2" id="link-banks">

At [bridge.simplefin.org](https://bridge.simplefin.org), create an account and connect each bank you
want covered. That is where every bank login lives: you sign in to your banks at SimpleFIN, and
Postern never sees a bank credential — not during this setup, and not afterwards.

The banks you link there are the banks this connection carries. SimpleFIN arrives in Postern as one
connection holding every institution behind it, so adding a bank later, or dropping one, is done at
the Bridge and shows up here on the next sync. There is nothing in the Console that picks banks.

The subscription is billed by SimpleFIN and stays yours whether or not Postern is running.

</Step>

<Step title="Copy the setup token" titleSize="h2" id="setup-token">

Still at the Bridge, request an access URL. What it hands back is a setup token — a long base64
string on one line. Copy the whole thing: it carries no spaces and no line breaks, and a truncated
paste fails the same way a wrong one does.

You are holding a credential from this point on. Postern will convert it into the durable one in the
next step; treat both the way you would treat a bank password until then.

</Step>

<Step title="Paste the token into the Console" titleSize="h2" id="paste-token">

Console → Sources → **Add a source** → **SimpleFIN**. Under **Paste it here** the card has one
field, labelled **Access URL**, with `https://…@bridge.simplefin.org/…` shown in it as a placeholder
— and it takes either form, so paste the setup token there if that is what you have.

<Warning>
  Pressing **Connect SimpleFIN** claims the setup token, and a setup token can be claimed exactly
  once. After a successful claim it is spent: pasting it again, here or into any other SimpleFIN
  client, fails. What Postern holds from then on is the access URL the Bridge returns in exchange —
  it embeds a username and password that read every bank you linked, Postern seals it in the local
  vault and never shows it back to you. If you pasted an access URL rather than a token, that string
  in your clipboard or your notes is that same credential; delete the copy you pasted from.
</Warning>

Press **Connect SimpleFIN**. Postern decodes the token into the claim address the Bridge put inside
it, claims it once over HTTPS, and seals the access URL that comes back — the setup token itself is
never stored. When the paste and the first read both succeed, a message reads `SimpleFIN connected.`
and the Console returns you to Sources with a SimpleFIN row on it.

If SimpleFIN is already connected, the card says so before you press anything —
`SimpleFIN is already connected. Connecting again replaces the stored credential — it won’t create a
second copy.` — and it holds the button until you tick the box beside it, which is the Console asking
whether you meant it. The button then reads **Reconnect SimpleFIN**.

<Note>
  The Console checks the shape of what you pasted before it spends a round trip, and answers in the
  field. An empty field answers `Paste your SimpleFIN access URL, or the setup token from the
  Bridge.` A bare bridge address answers `That’s the bridge address, not your access URL. Yours
  starts with https:// and carries a token after the //.` — a real access URL carries credentials
  between the `//` and the `@`, and `https://bridge.simplefin.org` on its own has no such segment.
  Neither is a refusal from SimpleFIN; nothing has been sent yet.
</Note>

<Note>
  The card answers `SimpleFIN refused it.` with `simplefin setup-token claim failed` printed
  underneath it. The claim did not go through. Every way a claim can fail collapses to that one
  message — an already-claimed token, a mangled or truncated paste, a token the Bridge no longer
  recognises, or a box that could not reach the Bridge at all — because Postern refuses to put any
  part of a credential into an error string. Nothing was written: the claim runs before the
  connection row, so a failed claim leaves no half-connected source behind. Get a fresh setup token
  from the Bridge, or paste the access URL if you kept it.
</Note>

</Step>

<Step title="Watch the first sync land" titleSize="h2" id="first-sync">

Back in Console → Sources, the connection has already read. Connecting does more than store a
credential: Postern runs the first sync straight away and reports how it went rather than swallowing
it, so the `SimpleFIN connected.` message means the first read worked as well.

That first sync asks the Bridge for a year of history, in 90-day windows laid end to end, because the
Bridge silently caps any span longer than 90 days. Every sync after it asks only for what has
happened since the last one plus a three-day overlap, which is what catches a transaction that posts
a couple of days late. Re-reading that overlap changes nothing already stored.

What lands is accounts with their balances, posted transactions, and investment positions for the
accounts that report them. Pending charges are not included — Postern asks the Bridge for posted
transactions only, so a charge appears when it settles rather than when it is authorised.

Console → Sources now carries a SimpleFIN row with an account count on it, and under that row the
institutions it covers: their names run together separated by ` · `, followed by
`One link carries all <n> — which banks are in it is chosen at SimpleFIN Bridge ↗`, carrying your own
number. With a single institution behind the link that line reads `One link carries it` instead.

<Note>
  The card stays where it is and reads `SimpleFIN is connected, but its first sync failed.`, with the
  gateway's own message underneath and a **Manage this connection →** link below that. The connection
  is real and nothing is rolled back — Postern will not delete a connection that genuinely exists to
  make a screen look tidier. Follow that link, or reach the same page from Sources, and press **Sync
  now**: it answers `Synced — <n> updated.` with its own count, or shows you the failure verbatim.
</Note>

<Note>
  A bank you linked at the Bridge is missing from the institutions line, and nothing looks broken. A
  partly-failing read comes back from the Bridge as a normal answer carrying per-institution error
  strings rather than as an error. Postern keeps everything that did arrive, writes each of those
  strings to its own log prefixed `simplefin:`, and skips the investment-position sweep for that
  round so that a bank which failed to answer does not have its holdings marked gone. `docker compose
  logs -f app` on the box that runs Postern carries those lines.
</Note>

</Step>

<Step title="Choose how often it re-syncs" titleSize="h2" id="cadence">

Console → Sources → the SimpleFIN row → **Manage** opens the connection. **Poll cadence** is a row of
chips there — 2h, 6h, 12h, 1d — and a new connection sits on 6h, which asks nothing of you if that
suits. Pick another and it takes effect without a restart: the scheduler re-reads its table about
once a minute and moves the timer, and the connection's next expected check moves with it.

2h is the floor, and the Console offers nothing below it. The gloss under the chips says so:
`How often Postern re-syncs this connection. Minimum 2h — a faster override is clamped to that
floor.` The connector declares that floor to stay inside the daily request budget the Bridge
publishes for its tokens, which it enforces by disabling tokens that go past it. Polling harder also
buys less than it looks like it should — the Bridge refreshes what it holds roughly daily, so most of
the extra reads return rows you already have.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Confirm it works

Four checks, in the Console. Together they show that the credential took, that a read actually
reached the Bridge, and that the coverage is the coverage you paid for.

- Console → Sources lists SimpleFIN with an account count on the row, rather than a zero.
- The line under that row names each bank you linked at the Bridge. A bank you expected and cannot
  see is either not linked at the Bridge or failed on this read.
- Open the connection from **Manage**: it was last heard from minutes ago rather than never, and one
  **Poll cadence** chip is selected.
- Press **Sync now** on that page. It answers `Synced — <n> updated.` with its own count. Run it
  again a minute later and a smaller number, or zero, is the correct answer — that is the three-day
  overlap re-reading what is already stored.

## What you have now

One finance connection carrying every bank the Bridge covers for you: accounts and balances, posted
transactions, and investment positions where the bank reports them, syncing on the cadence you chose
and answered from the local cache in milliseconds by any agent you have granted finance. Finance is
read-only — the connector declares no actions at all — so a finance grant is a grant to read.

Some of what arrives is shaped by the source, and it decides whether an agent's answer is right.
Transaction amounts are signed, with negative meaning money leaving the account, so a plain sum nets
spending against income. Nothing is categorised: SimpleFIN sends no categories, and an absent
category means *not categorised*, never *uncategorised spending*. Investment positions are a
snapshot of what is held now rather than a stream of trades, and a cost basis of `0.00` is SimpleFIN
saying *unknown*, not *free*.

The standing costs are the Bridge's, not Postern's. The subscription renews whether or not Postern
is running, and it is billed by SimpleFIN. Which banks this connection covers stays a decision you
make at the Bridge. Disconnecting in the Console — Sources → the connection → **Disconnect**, which
asks you to type `disconnect` before it does anything and tells you first that it
`Removes Finance's cached data and this connection. Re-add it anytime.` — deletes this connection's
cached rows, and with them the sealed access URL, because no other connection is using that
credential. Nothing is sent to the Bridge when you do: your subscription and your linked banks are
untouched, and the access URL stops being used by anything.

If you add Plaid later for a bank the Bridge cannot reach, the two do not double-count. Postern
matches an account arriving through both providers on the institution's domain and the last four
digits in the account name, and serves one.
